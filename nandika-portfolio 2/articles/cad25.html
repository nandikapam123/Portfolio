DECODE 2025–26: Shooter and Intake Design Calculations

What launch speed, hood angle, and flywheel inertia a repeatable 5 in artifact shooter needs, and how I will verify it.

Open the CAD model ↗ Calculated; not yet measured

Design problem

DECODE (FTC 2025–26) is a shooting game: robots collect and launch 5 in (12.70 cm) polypropylene balls, called artifacts, into elevated goals, and the robot must fit an 18 in cube at the start of a match. A shooter has to deliver one artifact at a time to a fixed target from a range of field positions, so the engineering question is which launch speed, angle and wheel inertia keep that repeatable. The CAD is in Onshape. Official season materials are on the FIRST game page.

Required exit speed

For a launch from height h0 at angle θ to a target Δh above it at horizontal distance x, ignoring drag and spin, v² = g x² / (2 cos²θ (x tanθ − Δh)). The hood angle therefore trades speed against descent angle at the goal: a steeper launch needs more speed at short range but arrives steeply, which helps clear a rim.

Flywheel droop and wheel inertia

A single wheel pressing the ball against a fixed hood launches it at about half the wheel’s surface speed (ideal, no slip), so the wheel must spin at ω = 2v/r. For a hollow ball rolling through that contact, the ball takes (5/6)·m·v² of energy (translation plus spin), and the wheel supplies it from E = ½Iω². The fractional speed drop per shot is 1 − √(1 − ΔE/E). A heavier wheel stores more energy, so droop falls, but spin-up time and mass rise.

Throughput

In a pipeline the cycle time is set by the slowest stage: T = max(Tintake, Ttransfer, Tspin-up + Tlaunch), so shots per minute are 60/T. Speeding a stage that is not the bottleneck changes nothing, which is why I time each stage separately before changing any of them.
