BIOBUZZ 2026–27: Dual Launchers and Passive Sorting, Design Calculations

Why a purely geometric sorter is a statistics problem, and what ramp slope the routing speed allows.

Open the CAD model ↗ Design in progress

Design problem

BIOBUZZ (FTC 2026–27) has two scoring elements: Pollen, plastic balls about 3 in in diameter according to FIRST’s game preview, and Nectar, which is larger and heavier. Alliances launch them into a hive’s cell to tip it. The current CAD (Onshape) uses two launchers, a compliant intake, passive sorting, and a turret. Two questions decide whether that architecture works: will a purely geometric sorter send each element to the right launcher, and is the routing fast enough?

Passive sorting under tolerance

A size-selective aperture of width a passes Pollen (diameter dP) and blocks Nectar (dN). If the diameters vary as normal with standard deviation σ, a piece is misrouted when Pollen is wider than a or Nectar narrower than a. Centering a with margin m on both sides gives P(error) = 2·Q(m/σ), where Q is the normal tail probability. The sorter’s reliability is therefore set by margin relative to σ, not by margin alone.

The second term matters most in practice: orientation, deformation of a compliant ball, and wear add to σ. The design rule I take from this is to choose the aperture from measured diameters of at least 30 pieces of each element, and to size the margin for a target error rate, not for the nominal sizes.

Routing speed on a ramp

A hollow ball rolling without slipping down a slope of angle θ accelerates at a = g sinθ / (1 + I/mr²), which is (3/5) g sinθ for a thin shell and (5/7) g sinθ for a solid sphere; sliding would give g sinθ. Rolling is slower than sliding, which sets the transit time on a fixed-length ramp, and so the cycle time of the sorter.

Aiming

The turret’s angular error converts to a lateral miss of d·tanδ at distance d. At 3 m a 1° error is 3 × tan 1° ≈ 52 mm, which is why backlash and homing repeatability are specified in degrees, not millimetres. Third-party summaries of the game mention AprilTag-based aiming; I treat that as a candidate approach to be checked against the manual and tested.
